The Story

This recipe was born from crisp autumn mornings and the scent of woodsmoke hanging in the air. It is a slow, deliberate process that requires patience but rewards you with deeply complex flavors. Passed down through three generations, it has been refined but never truly altered.

The secret lies not just in the ingredients, but in the rhythm of the preparation. Every step is an homage to the land it came from, utilizing seasonal produce at the peak of its ripeness.

Regional Origins

Rooted deeply in the agrarian traditions of the southern valleys, this dish reflects a history of making much out of little. The harsh winters dictated the need for preserving techniques, which are evident in the cured elements of this recipe.

Ingredients (Serves – 4)

San Marzano Tomatoes 800g — Canned or Fresh (Peeled)
Extra Virgin Olive Oil 60ml — Cold Pressed
Fresh Basil 1 Bunch — Hand-torn
Garlic Cloves 3 Large — Thinly Sliced

Method

01

Infuse the oil with garlic over low heat until fragrant but not browned.

Estimated Time: 5 mins
02

Add the tomatoes and simmer slowly, allowing the sauce to thicken naturally.

Traditional Tip

Never rush the reduction; the sweetness comes from the slow evaporation of water.
